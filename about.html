who am i

I've always lived in the and.

I think the most interesting people are the ones who resist the pressure to be one thing. Not because they're scattered — but because they understand that opposites don't cancel each other out. They compound. The contradiction is the point.

I write love stories. I study arrhythmias. I build startups. I also break things — sometimes deliberately, sometimes on a mountain. Both are data.

I don't think I'll ever be just one thing. I've stopped trying to be.

the long version

Some traumatic life events land me in an 8-month hospitalization. I miss a full year of school — one I never retook, which left real gaps I had to paper over for years. The most depressing year of my existence. I nearly lose my life and am genuinely unsure what it's all for. I am furious at the medical system and the incapabilities I watched up close — which forced me to take care of myself at 13. In one word: rage. Despite every odd stacked against me, I make it out. Rock bottom had a basement. My iron resilience and my family pulled me through. I never looked back.
2012
I feel a strange pull toward cardiac surgery and decide that's it. To get there I need to pass a brutal national entrance exam with a ruthlessly low acceptance rate. The odds aren't great — my concentration and cognitive stamina are still recovering, I missed so much school, support is thin outside family. I decide: I want it, I'll get it. I fail in ways I didn't know a person could fail. I keep going. De aanhouder wint.
2014
I graduate Medical School and enter the Surgical Residency Program. A long time coming.
2021–22
I get into the MIT Venture Advancement Program in Cambridge. For the first time I'm fully inside an entrepreneurial ecosystem — MIT, Harvard, people who build things from scratch. It lights something up. My love for creating and solving finds a name.
2023
I take a leap and apply to the Stanford Biodesign and Harvard fellowships in healthtech innovation. I prep ferociously — deep into imposter syndrome. Then I meet Gael, from a pitch comp, and she quietly changes my life. She helps me see I'm neurodivergent and that my authenticity isn't a weakness — it's my sharpest edge. I close my books. I decide to wing it. I'm in love with Stanford but apply to Harvard too, fully prepared to not get in. I get into both. A win after a long streak of failures — and the one that mattered most.
2024

about me

Rani is a Belgian MD, PhD candidate, former surgery resident, and Stanford Biodesign Fellow.

She graduated magna cum laude from VUB, where she joined Brugada's heart lab to study complications in arrhythmia ablation and investigate prevention strategies. She has co-authored over 20 peer-reviewed publications, first-authored two novel surgical techniques, and presented her work internationally.

Her PhD research focuses on finding eligible 3D printing materials for patient-tailored epicardial guides in hybrid arrhythmia ablation. She is currently an Innovation Fellow at Stanford Biodesign, focused on need-driven healthcare innovation.
